A music video

llmctl musicvideo song.flac shot1.png shot2.png ... cuts a song into sections, has the pictures sing each with LTX-2.5 Talking, cuts every clip to exactly the frames of its section and lays the whole song underneath — so the lips stay on the sound from the first frame to the last.

30 s of German synthpop

The song from ACE-Step 1.5 XL; the singer from Z-Image, two more shots of the same person from Qwen-Image Edit (every one from the original, never an edit of an edit). Six sections of 5 s, the shots taking turns; ~6.5 min a section, ~40 min in all. The lips follow the singing; close-ups follow better than head-and-shoulders.

Making one, step by step

The music video above, made again from nothing. Every step runs on the same machine; ComfyUI on slot 9, its image API (--proxy) on port 8089.

  1. The song. ACE-Step 1.5 XL Turbo through llmctl's /v1/audio/music: style tags, lyrics with [Verse]/[Chorus], tempo, key and language. 30 s in ~20 s.
  2. The singer. One picture with Z-Image Turbo: a close-up, the face filling much of the frame, 16:9 like the video.
  3. More shots of the same person. Qwen-Image Edit Turbo, every one edited from that first picture (an edit of an edit gathers noise): a medium shot at a microphone stand, a profile with eyes closed.
  4. The sections. Without a file the pictures take turns; with one (--sections, see examples/musicvideo.sections) each section gets its shot and its own prompt, or plays a scene instead of singing.
  5. Look before rendering. --dry-run shows the plan and the time it will take; then the render, ~6.5 min per 5 s section. Finished sections are kept beside the output, so a run that stops, or one with a few lines changed, renders only what is missing.

    What makes it work

    • Close-ups for lines with many words. With the face filling the frame the lips follow the singing closely; at head-and-shoulders they drift.
    • Wider shots and profiles for the chorus and for long notes, where the mouth matters less.
    • Scenes for instrumental parts (scene: … in the sections file): no singing, the song goes on underneath.
    • Every shot from the first picture. The same face in every section; an edit of an edit drifts and gathers grain.
    • --continuous starts each section on the last frame of the one before: one continuous take, but the face drifts a little with every section. For a story to a song without lip sync, a storyboard with the song as "soundtrack" keeps the person the same over far more seconds.
    • The song stays the song. The video's sound is the original track, not LTX's: the picture is cut to the song's frames, never the other way round.
